How it works

Bass Note Finder trains you to connect what you hear, what you read and where it sits on the neck.

  1. Listen. Tap “New note” and a random note (or a chord arpeggio in Chords mode) plays.
  2. Find it. Hunt it down on your bass until your note matches.
  3. Stuck? Tap 💡 Hint to see it written on the bass clef stave.
  4. Check. Tap Reveal to see the name and every place it lives on the fretboard, then mark whether you got it.

Intervals mode plays 2–4 notes from a key you choose. Name the interval between each pair (e.g. major 3rd up, perfect 4th down), then reveal the notes, their scale degrees and where they sit on the neck.

Listening: tap 🎤 and the app listens through your microphone. Play the note (or the chord or interval notes in order) and it marks you right automatically and moves on.

Hands-free mode does all of this on a timer so you can keep both hands on the instrument. With smart repetition on, anything that takes you 4 or more attempts (or a miss) plays again straight away so you can lock it in, anything that takes several tries comes back within a few rounds, and the ones you get first time come back less and less often, so practice time goes where you need it.

Install it: on iPhone tap Share → Add to Home Screen; on Android open the browser menu → Install app. Once installed it works offline. Your score and settings stay on your device.

Listening

Phone mics pick up very little of a bass’s lowest frequencies, so octaves are hard to tell apart. “Same note name” is reliable; use “Exact octave” with headphones and a good mic or an audio interface. Listening needs microphone permission and only works when the app is opened from a website over HTTPS.

Notation

“As a hint” keeps the stave hidden until you tap 💡 Hint (or the stave itself). Reveal always shows it too. “Straight away” shows the notation as the sound plays, for reading practice. Chord notes are spelled properly, so you may see things like E♯ or B♭♭.

Bass & sound

With +1 or +2 octave playback, any note that would sound higher than your bass can reach is played an octave lower instead.
Phone speakers can’t reproduce low bass. Use +1 octave on the speaker, “True bass” with headphones.
Natural notes onlyNo sharps or flats (applies to chord roots too)
